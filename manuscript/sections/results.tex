\subsection{Paired confirmatory comparisons}

Relative to the matched baseline, mean Pearson increased by
\LayerLinearPearsonDelta{} for the penultimate-layer linear probe,
\RichResidualPearsonDelta{} for the rich-statistics residual head, and
\MultiQueryPearsonDelta{} for the multi-query head. All three Holm-adjusted
one-sided p-values were below the predeclared threshold. Nevertheless, their
95\% hierarchical-bootstrap intervals extended below zero: the respective
lower bounds were \LayerLinearCILow{}, \RichResidualCILow{}, and

\subsection{Secondary capacity--data regime}

We next asked whether the external behavior of the richer heads changes with
the amount of HBN development data. The secondary matrix contains the
mean-linear baseline and two candidate heads at 200, 400, and 800 training
subjects, with ten fixed seeds per cell. It therefore contributes 90 training
runs and 6,750 external prediction identities, while preserving the same
sealed 75-subject MIPDB evaluation. Full aggregate tables and transfer details
are provided in the supplementary section.

Both candidate heads had positive mean paired Pearson differences at every
training size, and every observed seed-level difference was positive. However,
the paired advantage did not increase monotonically with the training subset:
the gains were smaller at 800 subjects than at 200 subjects, while the larger
regimes retained at least one candidate interval compatible with no difference.
Figure~\ref{fig:capacity-main}
summarizes this pattern. The result is therefore consistent with diminishing
or unstable returns from added development data under this protocol, not with a
universal scaling law or a general superiority claim for richer heads.

The exact seed sign-flip tests and Holm corrections reported for this extension
are descriptive exploratory inference. They do not modify the primary
predeclared stability rule, whose conclusion remains that no tested complex
head established a stable external gain.

\subsection{Secondary layer-wise analysis}

The layer-wise extension asked whether age information was more reliably
accessible before the final REVE layer without changing probe capacity. We used
the final-layer mean-linear probe as the baseline and compared matched linear
probes at layers $-4$, $-3$, and $-2$. The analysis covered four layers, ten
matched seeds, and the same \LayerwiseSubjectCount{}-subject sealed external
cohort. It was
exploratory and was not a primary superiority claim.

\subsection{Calibration}

All heads had mean calibration slopes above one, with the multi-query model
closest to the ideal slope among the four. Intercepts were also displaced from
zero. These patterns reinforce that a ranking by correlation is not a complete
assessment of the resulting age predictions.
