Frozen-probe scores reflect both an encoder and the protocol used to read out
its representations. We study how age decoding from frozen \REVE{} EEG
representations depends on the head, development-set size, evaluation metric,
and external uncertainty. In a predeclared comparison, four heads trained on
the same HBN development split with \ProbeSeeds{} optimization seeds were
evaluated on a sealed cohort of \PrimarySubjects{} MIPDB subjects. The
mean-linear baseline obtained Pearson correlation \BaselinePearson{}.
Candidate mean paired Pearson differences were \LayerLinearPearsonDelta{},
\RichResidualPearsonDelta{}, and \MultiQueryPearsonDelta{}, but every joint
seed--subject bootstrap interval included zero. Thus no candidate met the
predeclared stability criterion; this result does not establish equivalence.
Metric rankings differed descriptively: the rich-statistics residual head had
the highest mean Pearson, whereas the multi-query head had the lowest MAE and
RMSE. Secondary capacity--data experiments compared three heads at 200, 400,
and 800 HBN training subjects. Candidate advantages were positive at every size,
but their point estimates did not grow monotonically with training size.
Exploratory layer-wise comparisons likewise gave positive earlier-layer
differences with intervals that crossed zero. These extensions reused the same
external subjects and are not independent replications. The bounded practical
contribution is an evaluation example in which head ranking depends on the
metric, head contrasts depend on the training regime, and consistent seed
directions coexist with substantial external-subject uncertainty. Frozen
representation comparisons should therefore declare the readout and metric,
match training regimes, and report subject-aware uncertainty alongside seed
variation.
