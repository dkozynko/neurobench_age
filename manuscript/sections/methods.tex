\section{Methods}

\subsection{Frozen representation pipeline}

We used the \texttt{brain-bzh/reve-base} checkpoint with encoder parameters
frozen, evaluation mode enabled, and inference-mode extraction. EEG was mapped
to the ordered EGI HydroCel E1--E128 layout without spatial interpolation,
resampled to 200 Hz, band-pass filtered from 0.5 to 99.5 Hz, standardized with
a clamp of 15, and divided into non-overlapping two-second windows. For MIPDB,
the StandardScaler was fitted separately to all retained samples from each
subject's selected recording before windowing. This is target-free,
recording-level normalization and is part of the external estimand; it is not
a train-fitted global scaler. At most 120 seconds were retained per subject.
Windows were never allowed to cross MIPDB condition boundaries.
Representations from the last two transformer layers were materialized once and
content-addressed; all heads consumed these immutable caches.

\subsection{Probe heads}

The baseline averages final-layer tokens and applies one affine regressor. The
penultimate-layer linear probe makes the same readout from the preceding layer,
isolating layer choice without adding capacity. The rich-statistics residual
head adds a zero-initialized correction over per-feature standard deviation,
range, mean absolute deviation, and mean absolute magnitude. The multi-query
head uses two deterministic signed-basis attention queries and a
zero-initialized residual over weighted location, spread, range, deviation, and
magnitude statistics. Zero initialization makes both residual heads exactly
match the baseline prediction at initialization, so subsequent differences
arise through training rather than an unmatched initial function.

\subsection{Training and selection}

Each head was trained for seeds 33--42 using global seeded window shuffling,
AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw} with learning rate 1e-4 and weight decay
0.05, batch size 64, OneCycleLR \citep{smith2018superconvergence} with cosine
annealing, gradient clipping at norm 1, and mean-squared error. Training had a
maximum of 40 epochs and patience 7. For each seed and
head, the earliest checkpoint attaining the best HBN validation Pearson
correlation was selected. Neither MIPDB targets nor predictions influenced
training, checkpoint selection, candidate membership, or thresholds.

\subsection{External estimand and descriptive metrics}

For each candidate head and optimization seed, we computed subject-level Pearson
correlation on the primary external cohort. The primary seed-level contrast is
the candidate Pearson correlation minus the matched baseline correlation, and
the estimand is its arithmetic mean over the ten matched seeds. We also report
MAE, RMSE, R-squared, calibration
slope and intercept, the sample standard deviation across seed correlations,
the number of positive, zero, and negative seed contrasts, and the worst seed
contrast. These additional metrics are descriptive and do not replace the
predeclared decision rule.

\subsection{Uncertainty and decision rule}

The interval estimate uses 10,000 deterministic hierarchical paired bootstrap
iterations and reports percentile 95\% intervals. Each iteration resamples
seeds with replacement and subjects with replacement, preserving
candidate--baseline pairing. A paired one-sided seed-level sign-flip
permutation test enumerates all 1,024 sign assignments for the ten paired seed
deltas. Its p-value is exact conditional on the null-model assumption that the
seed-level signs are exchangeable or symmetric under no improvement; it is not
a design-based randomization of candidate labels. Raw p-values for the three
candidates are adjusted in a fixed order with Holm's step-down method.

A candidate establishes stable external improvement only if all conditions are
true: the primary cohort meets the 50-subject minimum; adjusted p is below
0.05; the
95\% bootstrap lower bound is above zero; at least eight of ten seeds improve;
and the worst seed delta is at least minus 0.01. This conjunction was fixed before
external analysis. Failure of any condition blocks a superiority claim.

\subsection{Secondary layer-wise analysis}

To examine whether the final-layer choice itself was decisive, we ran a
separate exploratory comparison using the same sealed external cohort, ten
optimization seeds, and the completed four-layer representation matrix. The
final-layer mean-linear probe (layer $-1$) was the baseline; matched linear
probes at layers $-4$, $-3$, and $-2$ were the three comparisons. The analysis
used the same hierarchical bootstrap and exact seed sign-flip procedures, with
Holm adjustment in the fixed order $-4$, $-3$, $-2$. It was specified after the
primary protocol and was not allowed to alter the primary stability decision.

\subsection{Secondary capacity--data analysis}

The secondary extension repeats the frozen-head protocol at HBN training sizes
of 200, 400, and 800 subjects for the mean-linear baseline, the rich-statistics
residual head, and the matched four-unit MLP residual head. Each cell uses the
same ten seeds and is evaluated on the same sealed 75-subject MIPDB cohort,
yielding 90 runs and 6,750 external prediction identities. The estimand is the
change in the paired candidate--baseline Pearson difference across training
sizes. We report a joint seed--subject bootstrap and separate exploratory exact
seed sign-flip families for the six cells and six training-size contrasts. These
secondary p-values are descriptive and cannot change the primary stable-
improvement decision.
