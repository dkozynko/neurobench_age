\section{Discussion}

The central result is a separation between favorable point estimates and
stable external evidence. Each candidate produced a positive mean paired
Pearson difference, and each passed the adjusted one-sided randomization
threshold. Two candidates improved on every observed seed. Yet the
hierarchical intervals remained compatible with zero once both seed and subject
variation were propagated. Under the predeclared conjunction, no tested complex
head therefore established a stable gain.

\paragraph{The metric changes the descriptive ranking.}
The rich-statistics residual head obtained mean Pearson
\RichResidualPearson{} with MAE \RichResidualMAE{} years, whereas the
multi-query head obtained Pearson \MultiQueryPearson{} with MAE
\MultiQueryMAE{} years (Table~\ref{tab:absolute-metrics}). Thus the highest
correlation and lowest absolute error did not identify the same readout.
Pearson measures linear association, not agreement in years; a favorable
correlation cannot replace an error or calibration assessment. These secondary
metrics are descriptive, not additional confirmatory wins, and were not used
to replace the original Pearson endpoint after evaluation.

Descriptively, the earlier-layer linear result is compatible with age-relevant
signal being accessible before the final REVE layer, and the rich-statistics
head is compatible with token dispersion and magnitude being useful to a
downstream predictor. The multi-query result has lower observed error metrics
without establishing a reliable Pearson advantage. None of these observations
warrants a general claim that a richer readout reveals a better representation.

\paragraph{The readout and training regime define the comparison.}
Probe capacity is part of the scientific intervention. A nonlinear head can
recover information that is present but not linearly accessible, but it can
also exploit development-set regularities that do not transfer. Matched seeds,
identical cached representations, common optimization, and paired external
subjects reduce avoidable comparison noise; they do not eliminate cohort
uncertainty.

The capacity--data extension sharpens this interpretation. The point estimates
of the richer heads' paired external advantage were smaller at 800 training
subjects than at 200, and both 800-subject intervals included zero. This pattern
is consistent with additional data improving the baseline and candidate
together, or with cross-cohort transfer limiting the comparison rather than head
capacity. Neither explanation is isolated by this experiment. In particular,
a positive interval at one size and a zero-crossing interval at another are
not by themselves evidence that the effects differ. The direct training-size
contrasts, rather than separate significance decisions, address that question.
The observed MIPDB maximum must not be used to choose a new training size and
then described as an untouched external test. With only three nested sizes,
this is a bounded observation, not evidence against scaling more generally.

The secondary layer-wise analysis provides a similarly bounded result. Earlier
layers showed positive paired point estimates relative to the final-layer
baseline, but the subject-aware intervals crossed zero for all three tested
depths. This is compatible with age-relevant information being available before
the final layer, yet it does not show that an earlier layer is reliably better
under this cohort, seed set, and linear probe.

\paragraph{Seed consistency is not independent cohort replication.}
Changing the seed interrogates training variability on the observed subjects;
it does not recruit new external subjects. All three analyses here reuse the
same MIPDB cohort. Their agreement can describe this cohort more fully but
cannot establish that the same pattern will recur in another population.
Likewise, a zero-crossing interval does not exclude a useful benefit: the
intervals permit both unfavorable and favorable effects, and no practical
equivalence margin was prespecified.

\paragraph{Implications for evaluating frozen representations.}
The concrete reporting recommendation is to declare the head, layer,
development-set size, and primary metric as part of the evaluation protocol;
compare heads within matched training regimes; and show paired subject-aware
intervals alongside repeated-seed summaries. Absolute error and calibration
should accompany correlation without silently becoming replacement endpoints.
These recommendations are illustrated by this controlled case, not established
as a new general theory of representation quality.

The next discriminating experiment is an independently recruited,
age-compatible external cohort evaluated with frozen checkpoints and a
prospectively specified transfer protocol. Such a replication could extend the
same investigation while remaining separate from the original primary test.
A second encoder would test encoder specificity, whereas a larger HBN training
set alone would not resolve the lack of independent external replication.
