\section{Conclusion}

In a sealed external comparison of frozen REVE age representations, more
expressive heads produced positive average paired Pearson differences but did
not establish stable improvement over a mean-pooled linear probe. The decisive
evidence was not the direction of the point estimates or adjusted tests alone;
it was that every hierarchical-bootstrap interval crossed zero. The study
therefore supports only a bounded robustness conclusion, not an equivalence
claim.

The secondary capacity-data analysis adds that the point estimates of the
tested richer heads' paired advantages did not grow monotonically with the
HBN training subset; this is not a general claim about the effect of more data.
The exploratory layer-wise analysis likewise found positive earlier-layer point
estimates but intervals compatible with no difference. Together, the results
support reporting probe capacity, representation depth, and development-set
size as part of the estimand, while keeping the claim limited to the declared
REVE, cohort, heads, and seeds. These analyses reuse the same external people
and do not constitute independent cohort replications.

For representation research, the practical lesson is to report a specified
readout under a specified data regime and metric, rather than a context-free
encoder score. Here the highest-correlation head was not the lowest-error head,
and favorable seed directions did not remove external-subject uncertainty.
Independent external replication is the next test of whether these bounded
observations extend beyond MIPDB; the ds006780 transfer is encouraging but
precision-limited, and a new encoder is not required to perform the next
prospectively powered replication.
