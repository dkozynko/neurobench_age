\section{Conclusion}

In a sealed external comparison of frozen REVE age representations, more
expressive heads produced positive average paired Pearson differences but did
not establish stable improvement over a mean-pooled linear probe. The decisive
evidence was not the direction of the point estimates or adjusted tests alone;
it was that every hierarchical-bootstrap interval crossed zero. The study
therefore supports a bounded conclusion about robustness, not equivalence.

For representation research, the practical lesson is that probe expressivity,
seed variation, subject uncertainty, and dataset shift must be evaluated
together. A richer head can improve selected metrics without yielding evidence
that generalizes reliably enough to attribute the gain to the frozen
representation.
